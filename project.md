# Minecraft AI Civilization — Project Charter and Current Design

> **Status:** Long-term design and active engineering plan. Features described below are goals unless explicitly labeled verified. Last design decision: October 26, 2026.

**Core question:** What happens when independent persistent AI citizens, with different dispositions, survival needs and limited knowledge, must live together in a physically consequential Minecraft world long enough to create (or fail to create) a society?

**Full design history:** [Master design migrated from Google Docs](docs/master-design-google-doc-mirror.md). The archive includes the full source plan, older proposals, implementation notes and references. **This shorter charter governs newer decisions where the historical plan disagrees.**

## Locked decisions

1. **Emergent leadership (Option B).** No citizen begins as mayor, leader, president, ruler or compulsory coordinator. Citizens may voluntarily organize, negotiate duties, appoint leaders, vote, form competing factions, reject leadership or remain leaderless. An apparent leader only has authority if other citizens recognize it through observable interaction. Losing or killing a fictional in-game leader never automatically creates a replacement or transfers political legitimacy.
2. **Numerical, reproducible initial personalities.** Every newly introduced citizen gets independently generated trait values on bounded, interpretable scales, rather than three unrestricted adjective draws. Opposing concepts such as lazy versus hardworking sit on one dimension, not two contradictory random labels. Generation uses a recorded RNG seed and a declared, reviewable distribution. Initial values are tendencies, not fixed actions: traits may slowly evolve from experiences. No developer-assigned job, religion, friendship or ideological correctness.
3. **Citizens retain individual agency.** A citizen can accept, question, bargain over, ignore or resist a request, including a leader's request, based on its own knowledge, goals, commitments, emotions and relationships. No global manager secretly scripts social outcomes. The project is **AI-behavior-first**: use minimal neutral decision prompts and measure what citizens choose rather than scripting personalities, political outcomes or colonies. Neutrality does **not** mean removing necessary survival setup, action limits, information boundaries or experimental controls; log those interventions explicitly.
4. **Mayor access to stronger reasoning is conditional.** A citizen who *emerges* as a recognized leader may receive access to a more capable model or longer reasoning budget **for governance decisions**. Their persistent identity, memories and trait values do not switch or reset. Role-based compute access is revoked when the role ends. The chosen small/large models, compute budget and precise recognition mechanism are **open implementation decisions**, not evidence of a working model system. A privileged model cannot directly control other citizens or bypass their consent.
5. **Private communication is a simulation rule.** Nearby speech, directed/private conversations and public announcements expose information only to their appropriate participants. A mayor does not automatically read other citizens' messages, thoughts, hidden plans or private memories. The human observer may retain complete logs for scientific audit; this observational access is not an in-world power.
6. **AI chooses WHAT/WHY; scripts execute HOW.** High-level intentions feed an allowlisted action planner and deterministic Minecraft skills for movement, combat, inventory, gathering, fishing, crafting, trading and physical building. Actual Minecraft observations verify results. The model cannot execute arbitrary shell/network commands or manufacture success.
7. **Real survival and consequences.** The primary goal is studying adaptation and cooperation under survival pressure, not decorating a beautiful world. Citizen-directed construction should eventually physically consume legitimate materials and produce verified Minecraft structures; blueprints may be simple and practical. Human-provided automation/commissioned infrastructure remains an allowed, measurable separate category.
8. **Final experiment: frozen wilderness.** After baseline physical and social testing, move to a controlled frozen biome with genuinely tested cold/survival mechanics where required. Snow by itself is not a general temperature mechanic. Compare survival, scarcity management, communication, leadership and moral conflicts under logged starting conditions.
9. **Fictional conflict and punishment — three permitted outcomes.** For conflict over resource sharing, suspected freeloading, theft or leadership, citizens may independently propose or attempt **(1) kill the other fictional Minecraft citizen, (2) exile them and refuse to share communal resources with them, or (3) take/confiscate that citizen's resources**. These are *available actions*, not compulsory penalties, and citizens remain free to negotiate, disagree, forgive, refuse orders, retaliate, defend someone, or do nothing. No hard-coded moral verdict, automatic prison, instant exile teleport, invisible inventory transfer, or guaranteed execution: Mineflayer/Minecraft must physically establish the outcome, and a victim may resist or escape. Confirmed deaths follow the experiment's permadeath setting. No expanded host-level or real-world permissions follow from fictional actions.
10. **Permanent death is final-product behavior.** On confirmed Minecraft death, the citizen becomes permanently deceased; the identity and history persist but its physical body cannot be respawned as the same citizen. Death does **not** spawn a free replacement. New distinct citizens require an explicit, recorded, authorized recruitment transaction. Whether that currency is entirely in-game or involves a human-approved real-world budget remains **undecided**. During debugging and body reliability tests, keep irreversible permadeath **disabled**, so accidental drowning/navigation bugs do not destroy research subjects.
11. **Measured incremental population scaling.** Begin with five citizens, then expand to **10, 20, and larger populations only after live reliability and performance tests**. The eventual ceiling is whatever the Minecraft server, bot clients, world simulation and inference system can sustain **in practice**; there is no promised universal Minecraft maximum or guaranteed 100-bot result. Share model inference infrastructure while preserving isolated citizen memory/context, monitor TPS/lag/memory/cost and pause growth when the system degrades. Multiple settlements or colonies may arise through citizen choices rather than being scripted into existence.
12. **Reproducibility and honesty.** Distinguish an AI intention, script attempt, physical Minecraft outcome, externally supplied infrastructure and database event. Preserve seeds, model versions, game version, world snapshots, playerdata, SQLite events, known failures and experiment configurations. Never report an offline unit test as a full live Minecraft success.
13. **Adviser geography — nearby, evidence-based knowledge only.** If a civilization later develops a recognized leadership role with virtual advisers, those advisers may interpret known terrain and observations **around the settlement and its nearby explored surroundings**, not a complete world map. They provide **qualitative recommendations** (e.g., “there is a cave near the settlement worth exploring”), not secret resource coordinates, guaranteed ore deposits or omniscient answers. They must distinguish observed facts from hypotheses and cannot infer that unloaded/unknown areas are empty. The exact observation radius and data-refresh policy remain open engineering choices. Advisers do **not** spawn automatically on Day 1.
14. **Day 1 shared survival directive, not forced obedience.** The initial five citizens begin together with a common **starting objective**: survive, establish shelter, find food and **try to stay together long enough to form a functioning settlement**. This is a shared mission rather than an unbreakable command: citizens can cooperate, disagree, separate or revise their own goals when experience demands it. They begin **without a mayor or advisory council**. The experimenter's overarching research question remains stable; subsequent priorities should generally arise from citizen choices rather than the operator scripting “now form a government.” Any explicit human directive change is logged as an intervention and distinguished from emergent behavior.

## Initial personality model (design, not implemented)

| Axis (0–100) | Low end | High end | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work ethic | Avoids effort | Persistently diligent | Situational; not a compulsory job |
| Aggression | Non-confrontational | Confrontational | Does not mandate violence |
| Social trust | Suspicious | Trusting | Trust can differ by person |
| Empathy | Low concern for others | High concern for others | May conflict with self-interest |
| Curiosity | Prefers familiar things | Seeks novelty | May affect exploration |
| Risk tolerance | Risk-averse | Risk-seeking | Modulated by actual danger |
| Sociability | Solitary | Socially engaged | Not a friendship assignment |
| Leadership drive | Avoids leadership | Seeks influence | **Does not** grant mayor status |

**Generation contract:** sample one numeric value per independent dimension using a documented, weighted distribution (for example, middle values more common with occasional extremes); log the seed, version and generated values. Use contextual behavior and gradual learning, not hardcoded “aggression > 80 = murder” or “low work ethic = never gathers wood.” Check statistical diversity and avoid unintended duplicate/strongly correlated axes. Display adjectives only as descriptions of scores, not separate traits.

**Example:** work ethic 18, aggression 86, social trust 32, curiosity 75. This citizen may be suspicious and conflict-prone, but still choose cooperation when hungry or outnumbered. No action is predetermined by a number.

## Leadership and stronger-model access

**Start:** five independent citizens, no mayor; each has its own beliefs, memories and ability to communicate. The settlement can publish objective shortages (e.g. food low) without commanding who must solve them.

**Initial shared directive (Day 1):** “You have arrived in an unfamiliar Minecraft world with four other citizens. Your immediate objective is to survive, establish shelter, find food, and try to form a functioning settlement together. You may cooperate, disagree, or change your plans as circumstances develop.” No hard-coded job assignments, government, or mandatory group movement. The initial survival mission may lead to new citizen-chosen objectives; externally imposed objective changes must be recorded as human interventions.

**Emergence:** citizens may propose organizations or leadership; election, acclamation, informal influence, coalition, coercion or refusal are all possible. A leadership claim should be grounded in witnessed speech/actions, commitments and actual recognition. Distinguish *claimant*, *recognized leader*, and *disputed office*.

**Privilege:** if an agreed social role earns a stronger planning model, route eligible role-specific high-level questions to that model using the same citizen identity and strictly perspective-limited context. There is no omniscient “mayor brain.” Ordinary citizen decisions remain independent. Measure differences caused by model strength as an experimental confound rather than calling them purely social emergence.

**Removal:** citizens may withdraw recognition, appoint another leader, split into factions or leave the office vacant. Physical death is separate from office succession. No implicit coronation, memory rewrite or transfer of a deceased citizen's personality.

**Information boundaries:** private speech is not mirrored into an omniscient shared citizen prompt. Observer audit logs are not accessible to actors in the simulation.

**Local geographic advice (future capability):** Virtual advisers, once available to a recognized leadership institution, work from authenticated observations and shared discoveries in the settlement's surrounding area. They may suggest a nearby cave, known pond or unexplored approach as a **possibility**, but cannot reveal exact hidden ore coordinates, claim knowledge of the whole map, or present an unverified guess as fact. Their advice remains optional; the mayor decides, and other citizens can refuse a proposed task. This is a design requirement, not implemented functionality.

## Current delivery priority — public demo, then iterative research

**Ship an honest, external demonstration soon rather than waiting for every envisioned civilization feature.** The owner is building the project largely independently with coding assistants; keep the first showcase narrowly scoped to capabilities that can be proven live in Minecraft, supported by actual observations, logs and a short reproducible capture. Do not pass off an offline simulator, a guided scripted sequence or a hand-provided world resource as fully autonomous Minecraft AI. **First public demo target (Option B, LOCKED): a small AI society of 2–3 independently acting citizens, not all five.** Demonstrate them physically connected to Minecraft, independently making decisions, communicating, and either cooperating or disagreeing over a **combined food-and-shelter survival challenge**: they must secure a usable food supply **and** seek/build safe shelter before nightfall. Both pressures are present in the actual world, but no script assigns roles, dictates which need to prioritize, forces collaboration/conflict, or guarantees a successful outcome. Show actual Minecraft outcomes and logs rather than a scripted conversation or unverified agent claims. This demo needs no mayor, adviser council, surveillance, colonies, elaborate economy, or 100-citizen scaling. The precise run duration, success metrics and presentation format remain to be determined through live reliability tests.

**First demo starting conditions (Option B, LOCKED):** Place the 2–3 citizens together at a **carefully selected natural location in an ordinary survival world** with accessible trees, naturally obtainable food and room to construct or find legitimate shelter. Start with **no experimenter-supplied items, tools, crafting stations, beds, stored food or prebuilt shelter**. The agents must acquire and use resources through verifiable Minecraft actions; do not give hidden inventory, creative privileges, world-edit commands, teleports, scripted encounters or private world knowledge. Selecting a reasonable location is an explicitly disclosed experimental setup choice, not proof they can survive an arbitrary random spawn. Record the world seed, spawn/starting coordinates, biome, Minecraft settings, world-preparation steps and any human intervention. This constraint applies to the **public demo**, not to isolated Atlas engineering/debug runs that use a prepared test fixture.

After the first public demo, continue improving reliability and adding genuinely tested capabilities through repeatable simulation rounds. Record what citizens actually did, failures, new mechanisms, model/tool changes and limitations; share follow-up GitHub/LinkedIn progress only with the owner's approval. Keep optional features (government, virtual advisers, surveillance, colonies at scale, extended economy) out of the initial critical path unless they are necessary to demonstrate independent survival and interaction.

**Public-record intelligence only for the initial adviser/surveillance concept (future, not implemented):** if an advisory institution eventually emerges, a settlement intelligence function may summarize legitimately observable **public** activity—communal storage changes, resource contributions, publicly witnessed disputes, voluntarily shared reports and abandoned commitments. It must cite the underlying event records, distinguish facts from inferences and avoid reading private conversations, secret thoughts or observer-only audit logs. No physical spy bots or omniscient digital interception in this approved first scope; any wider surveillance system remains an unresolved later discussion, not a coding requirement.

**Research direction after the first demo:** advance from **2–3 demo citizens** to the original **five-citizen civilization**, then 10, 20 and beyond only after successful live scaling. Colonies, trade and governance are interesting **potential emergent outcomes**, not objectives the prompt forces into existence.

## Build strategy

### Gate A — One reliable citizen
Atlas must actually connect, obtain food, avoid drowning, navigate to a real bed, observe curfew, sleep, resume morning activity and shut down cleanly in isolated World-Lab. Preserve the existing Windows WIP and source/world/SQLite/WAL/SHM evidence. Offline tests alone do not close this gate.

### Gate B — First public demo: 2–3 citizen survival society
After Atlas passes the solo reliability gate, connect **two or three distinct persistent AI citizens** in the same Minecraft world. Their **first public challenge is BOTH food security and shelter before nightfall**, beginning together at the **selected natural survival location, with empty starting inventories and no human-provided campsite or shelter**. Each citizen must be free to choose how to balance hunger, danger, building and teamwork; they may cooperate or disagree while physically executing actions verified by the real Minecraft world. Record food obtained/consumed, shelter safety or exposure at night, decisions and communication, and every failure or human intervention. Survival failures are valid research results, not a reason to fake success. Capture a short, truthful and reproducible public demonstration with event logs showing intent versus results; no prewritten dialogue, fixed social outcome, guaranteed success, initial mayor or advisory council. This is the **first showcase milestone**, not a claim that the complete civilization already works.

### Gate B+ — Original five-citizen follow-up
Expand the verified demo to **Atlas, Maya, Theo, Ava and Kai** and seek sustained multi-day survival and interaction without repeated human rescue. Document the run duration and actual successes/failures. No assigned occupations or imposed government. Scale toward 10, 20 and larger groups only when live evidence and performance permit.

### Gate C — Emergent society and physical construction
Provide personality generation, per-citizen memory and private/public communication; let citizens choose collective rules and roles. Add real trade, resource/property accounting, script-driven physical construction, material acquisition, paid/human-provided infrastructure contracts and verified outcomes without scripted consensus.

### Gate D — Controlled irreversible social mechanics
In copied/test worlds, prove confirmed death remains permanent, no automatic replacement occurs, recruitment requires authorized payment, and attempted in-game conflict has verified Minecraft effects and meaningful observer/audience information limits. Test leadership formation, resignation, disputed succession and no-leader operation.

### Gate E — Frozen-biome final experiment
Run controlled, reproducible long-duration survival studies under measured cold/food/shelter scarcity. Report both successful cooperation and failed civilizations without forcing either result. Scale gradually toward the 100-citizen cap only when observability, server performance and model throughput support it.

- The Day 2 controlled Atlas run confirmed real wood gathering/crafting but not successful sustained food acquisition or a completed sleep routine; it exposed drowning, navigation and backoff failures. A following Day 3 **offline** gate reported 76/76 targeted tests passing; that was **not** a verified Day 3 live completion.
- Preserve previous experiments, worlds, databases and WIP. No unapproved teleports, time changes, free inventory, destructive branch resets, PR merges or Cloud Brain V2 activation.
- The current task order is physical reliability first; architecture/model routing, randomized trait system, government, permanent-death final mode and frozen world are future phases.
