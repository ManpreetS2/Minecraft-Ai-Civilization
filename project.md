# Minecraft AI Civilization — Project Charter and Current Design

> **Status:** Long-term design and active engineering plan. Features described below are goals unless explicitly labeled verified. Current locked first-demo direction: October 31, 2026 — 2v2 emergent rival civilizations. Dated checkpoints below are historical snapshots, not continuously verified status.

## 🚨 PRIMARY PLATFORM POLICY — WINDOWS FIRST (OWNER-LOCKED NOVEMBER 13, 2026)

**WINDOWS HAS ALWAYS BEEN THE PRIMARY DEVELOPMENT, INTEGRATION, TESTING, AND MINECRAFT PAPER EXECUTION PLATFORM FOR THIS PROJECT. macOS IS ONLY A SECONDARY DEVELOPMENT / EXPERIMENTAL TEST GROUND — NOT THE REQUIRED TEST HOST.**

**Binding requirements:** The primary acceptance path, including Gate A disposable-world Paper smoke and subsequent real 1v1/2v2 verification, must work on the owner's Windows machine. macOS may be used for supplementary development, isolated experiments, and cross-platform checks; macOS-only tooling or QA is **not** a substitute for Windows acceptance and must **never** silently turn into a mandatory gate. Any older macOS-only operator package is a temporary limitation to be repaired for Windows, **not** a change of project platform.

**Safety stays mandatory on Windows:** Port process ownership, working-directory / `session.lock` verification, disposable-world isolation, evidence provenance, credential/budget safeguards, and fail-closed refusal behavior to an independently reviewed Windows-native implementation. **Do not bypass or weaken security checks to make the smoke run.** No real Paper execution, paid inference, protected-world changes, or protected merges are authorized solely by this platform decision; retain the normal explicit authorization and evidence gates.

**Precedence:** This owner-locked Windows-first policy overrides any conflicting platform assumptions in earlier agent prompts, macOS-focused smoke instructions, draft PRs, or archived plans. Do not require the owner to switch to a Mac to test the project.

**Core question:** What happens when independent persistent AI citizens, with different dispositions, survival needs and limited knowledge, must live together in a physically consequential Minecraft world long enough to create (or fail to create) a society?

**Full design history:** [Master design migrated from Google Docs](docs/master-design-google-doc-mirror.md). The archive includes the full source plan, older proposals, implementation notes and references. **This shorter charter governs newer decisions where the historical plan disagrees.**

## Active first-demo specification — Rival AI Civilizations (Option A; LOCKED October 31, 2026)

**Status: design approved; portions of the independent-agent and community-isolation runtime exist in unmerged draft branches, but no complete 2v2 runtime is merged on public `main` or live verified.** The first public demo is now **2 vs. 2: two GPT-family citizens and two Claude-family citizens**, each an independent agent with its own persistent model session, identity, personality, knowledge, memory and freedom of action. These are **two starting communities, not two hive minds or fixed enemy factions**. They may trade, coexist, ignore, fight, cooperate, migrate or change affiliation voluntarily. No forced leader, role, war, rivalry, trade, first contact, winner or government. **Option A means competition may emerge from real circumstances, not instructions.**

**One survival world; two fair starting locations.** The groups start separately at reasonably comparable natural sites, tentatively around **120–200 traversable blocks apart** (distance is a design starting point, not an inflexible requirement). Each citizen starts with **empty inventory**, no gifted items, equipment, beds, food, shelters or structures. The positions, biomes, seed and preparation decisions are logged; pre-run administrative positioning is disclosed. During the timed run, no human rescue, construction, teleporting, creative supplies, accelerated Minecraft time or script-forced encounter.

**Information and autonomy:** Use equivalent neutral survival/agency prompts, not "beat the other team." No group begins knowing the other settlement's location or private history unless it learns it through legitimate game observation or shared speech. Nearby speech, private messages and published facts must respect recipients and hearing limits. YC's agent team directory and mailbox **must be adapted** if they implicitly disclose the opposing group, enable global chat, leak secrets or enforce a common plan. Starting community membership need not remain permanent; changing affiliation does **not** switch the agent's underlying model or erase memory.

**Public acceptance target:** Four independent persistent citizens, natural food/shelter pressures, legal world-verified Minecraft gathering and construction where they choose, and **60 continuous real-world minutes** at normal day/night settings, with actual ticks/TPS, communication/contact evidence, death/failure data, full costs, and recorded interventions. Use **normal demo respawning** with `keepInventory=false`, preserving citizen identity/memory on death; actual permadeath remains post-demo. No guarantee the communities meet, develop infrastructure or survive; absence of conflict or contact is valid data. A single showcase does not rank GPT vs Claude or prove social emergence.

**Milestones now locked:** (0) preserve existing Windows WIP/worlds/database and safely evaluate the YC harness (full/partial/no adoption based on live benchmarks); (1) **1 GPT vs 1 Claude** independent-agent technical trial, including survival, model routing, memory, message privacy and persistence; (2) **2v2 / 60-minute** public rival-community demonstration; (3) later **3v3, longer/repeated trials** with counterbalanced sites, roster order, traits and world seeds; (4) later economy, social institutions, human-contractor construction, migration, long-term history and controlled conflict/permadeath. The former **Atlas/Maya/Theo three-citizen one-settlement first-demo plan is superseded**, archived as design history and **not an additional prerequisite**. Preserve existing citizen identities/data; their allocation in the 2v2 roster and the exact provider models remain open.

**Scope limits and confidence:** The YC harness is a **preferred experimental candidate, not an installed dependency**. It is a full Pi-based agent runtime, with potential sandbox and version-compatibility risks; don't run unsafe shell access or replace local WIP without proofs and backups. Model inference must be explicitly authenticated and usage-limited: **subscription-backed access is preferred where officially supported and verified**, with metered API access as a provider-specific fallback. Consumer subscriptions are not interchangeable with API keys, unlimited inference or permission to automate chat websites. Keep the original scientific honesty, observed-vs-claimed outcomes, visibility and no-scripted-society rules. The prior **3–6 focused-week** guess for a 2v2 showcase is only a speculative estimate until real integration tests; make no completion guarantee. **Owner intends to add details later; do not expand the V1 feature scope without a new decision.**

### Research differentiators — outperform the evidence, not the agent count (November 1, 2026)

**Strategic objective, not a claim of superiority:** The aim is to produce **more scientifically convincing evidence about autonomous social behavior and persistence** than a larger agent-count demonstration, not to promise that a four-citizen project will outperform YC's implementation. YC/Olam Labs' [Agents in Games research](https://labs.ycombinator.com/projects/games/) and [open-source Minecraft harness](https://github.com/yc-software/minecraft-agent-harness) motivate comparison; upstream descriptions or video footage are **not our experiment results**. Other agent-civilization projects and research also overlap with two-model communities; the premise alone is not unique.

**Two foundational proof obligations for the currently locked 2v2 V1 (P0, already within its scope):**
1. **Independent knowledge and event provenance.** The observer can record complete Minecraft truth, but each citizen must receive only information it witnessed, legitimately heard, or was explicitly told. Distinguish **world event → citizen observation or received report → citizen belief → decision → actual physical outcome**; a communicated allegation is not automatically a verified fact. Test negative cases: an unobserved chest transfer, undisclosed other-settlement location, private DM, or unauthorized event **must not** reach another citizen's prompt, persistent memory, summary or `observed_fact` record. This extends existing privacy/filtered-bulletin requirements, **not a new broad memory-system rewrite**. Treat any confirmed cross-citizen information leak as a blocking defect before claiming independent societies.
2. **Grounded survival and accounting.** Verify food physically obtained and consumed, hunger and health change, deaths, blocks mined/placed, viable shelter and real game time; distinguish a model's plan or claim from successful game actions. Record retries, stuck loops, failsafes and external interference. **Basic food/shelter survival is already a first-demo gate; a fully renewable, self-sustaining food economy is a valuable follow-up experiment, not mandatory proof in the first 60 minutes.**

**Follow-up research differentiators (P1, explicitly NOT first-demo blockers):**
3. **Natural diplomacy across communities.** After legitimate discovery, observe whether citizens choose to share information, offer help, trade, negotiate, avoid, defect or fight. Do not inject mandatory peace talks, encounters, hostile objectives, artificial ownership or predetermined alliances. Absence of contact remains valid data.
4. **Memory with measurable behavioral consequences.** Persist independently witnessed help, betrayal, promise, resource exchange and prior mistakes; ask whether a **later decision** plausibly depends on a particular memory, not whether the agent merely refers to it. In repeated controlled *follow-up* experiments, compare access to a relevant memory versus a suitably matched memory-absent condition; account for seeds, personalities and stochastic behavior. Never fabricate a social narrative from one anecdote.
5. **Reproducible comparative evidence.** Preserve exact model/version, prompt, tools, random seed, terrain/start conditions, roster order, token/cost budgets, experimental interventions, raw logs, world/inventory changes and failure cases. Counterbalance starting side and citizen ordering and run multiple trials before making any GPT-versus-Claude comparison. Prefer bounded hypotheses with both positive and negative outcomes over a single winner score or edited livestream highlights.

**Explicit priority rule:** **First: safe YC harness feasibility → individual independence/knowledge isolation → real survival → 60-minute 2v2 demonstration.** Later: renewable-food sustainability, remembered social consequences, voluntary diplomacy, 3v3/multi-hour and controlled ablations. Do **not** enlarge the locked V1 feature set, impose a scripted rivalry, redesign the dashboard, add compulsory factions/economy/government, or schedule extra API-consuming tests solely to compete with YC. This is a planning and measurement direction; none of these outcomes is currently claimed as implemented or experimentally proven in our project.

## Locked decisions

1. **Emergent leadership (Option B).** No citizen begins as mayor, leader, president, ruler or compulsory coordinator. Citizens may voluntarily organize, negotiate duties, appoint leaders, vote, form competing factions, reject leadership or remain leaderless. An apparent leader only has authority if other citizens recognize it through observable interaction. Losing or killing a fictional in-game leader never automatically creates a replacement or transfers political legitimacy.
2. **Numerical, reproducible initial personalities.** Every newly introduced citizen gets independently generated trait values on bounded, interpretable scales, rather than three unrestricted adjective draws. Opposing concepts such as lazy versus hardworking sit on one dimension, not two contradictory random labels. Generation uses a recorded RNG seed and a declared, reviewable distribution. Initial values are tendencies, not fixed actions: traits may slowly evolve from experiences. No developer-assigned job, religion, friendship or ideological correctness.
3. **Citizens retain individual agency.** A citizen can accept, question, bargain over, ignore or resist a request, including a leader's request, based on its own knowledge, goals, commitments, emotions and relationships. No global manager secretly scripts social outcomes. The project is **AI-behavior-first**: use minimal neutral decision prompts and measure what citizens choose rather than scripting personalities, political outcomes or colonies. Neutrality does **not** mean removing necessary survival setup, action limits, information boundaries or experimental controls; log those interventions explicitly.
4. **Mayor access to stronger reasoning is conditional.** A citizen who *emerges* as a recognized leader may receive access to a more capable model or longer reasoning budget **for governance decisions**. Their persistent identity, memories and trait values do not switch or reset. Role-based compute access is revoked when the role ends. The chosen small/large models, compute budget and precise recognition mechanism are **open implementation decisions**, not evidence of a working model system. A privileged model cannot directly control other citizens or bypass their consent.
5. **Private communication is a simulation rule.** Nearby speech, directed/private conversations and public announcements expose information only to their appropriate participants. A mayor does not automatically read other citizens' messages, thoughts, hidden plans or private memories. The human observer may retain complete logs for scientific audit; this observational access is not an in-world power.
6. **AI chooses WHAT/WHY; scripts execute HOW.** High-level intentions feed an allowlisted action planner and deterministic Minecraft skills for movement, combat, inventory, gathering, fishing, crafting, trading and physical building. Actual Minecraft observations verify results. The model cannot execute arbitrary shell/network commands or manufacture success. For the initial public demo, emergency shelter/evasion is allowed only as a transparent, safety-checked, bounded **last-resort fail-safe**, not a routine scripted shelter strategy; document it distinctly from model decisions.
7. **Real survival and consequences.** The primary goal is studying adaptation and cooperation under survival pressure, not decorating a beautiful world. Citizen-directed construction should eventually physically consume legitimate materials and produce verified Minecraft structures; blueprints may be simple and practical. Human-provided automation/commissioned infrastructure remains an allowed, measurable separate category.
8. **Final experiment: frozen wilderness.** After baseline physical and social testing, move to a controlled frozen biome with genuinely tested cold/survival mechanics where required. Snow by itself is not a general temperature mechanic. Compare survival, scarcity management, communication, leadership and moral conflicts under logged starting conditions.
9. **Fictional conflict and punishment — three permitted outcomes.** For conflict over resource sharing, suspected freeloading, theft or leadership, citizens may independently propose or attempt **(1) kill the other fictional Minecraft citizen, (2) exile them and refuse to share communal resources with them, or (3) take/confiscate that citizen's resources**. These are *available actions*, not compulsory penalties, and citizens remain free to negotiate, disagree, forgive, refuse orders, retaliate, defend someone, or do nothing. No hard-coded moral verdict, automatic prison, instant exile teleport, invisible inventory transfer, or guaranteed execution: Mineflayer/Minecraft must physically establish the outcome, and a victim may resist or escape. Confirmed deaths follow the experiment's permadeath setting. No expanded host-level or real-world permissions follow from fictional actions.
10. **Permanent death is final-product behavior.** On confirmed Minecraft death, the citizen becomes permanently deceased; the identity and history persist but its physical body cannot be respawned as the same citizen. Death does **not** spawn a free replacement. New distinct citizens require an explicit, recorded, authorized recruitment transaction. Whether that currency is entirely in-game or involves a human-approved real-world budget remains **undecided**. **Death-policy phases (LOCKED):** During engineering tests **and the first 60-minute public 2v2 rival-community demo**, use normal Minecraft survival respawning (vanilla item drops/loss; `keepInventory` must remain off). The same AI citizen keeps its identity, personality, memories and relationships across respawns; log every death, cause, dropped/recovered items and recovery behavior, without resetting or invisibly replacing the agent. In later **actual civilization experiments**, enable **permanent death** only after the required survival/navigation reliability is proven and the experiment starts with permadeath explicitly configured; a confirmed death ends that citizen's physical participation permanently, and replacements follow the separately approved recruitment rules. Do not confuse development/demo deaths with final experimental permadeath.
11. **Measured incremental population scaling.** Begin with the **1v1 technical trial**, then the locked **2v2 first demo**, then repeat/extend to **3v3** before scaling to **10, 20, and larger populations only after live reliability and performance tests**. The old five-citizen single-settlement plan remains historical, not a prerequisite. The eventual ceiling is whatever the Minecraft server, bot clients, world simulation and inference system can sustain **in practice**; there is no promised universal Minecraft maximum or guaranteed 100-bot result. Share model inference infrastructure while preserving isolated citizen memory/context, monitor TPS/lag/memory/cost and pause growth when the system degrades. Multiple settlements or colonies may arise through citizen choices rather than being scripted into existence.
12. **Reproducibility and honesty.** Distinguish an AI intention, script attempt, physical Minecraft outcome, externally supplied infrastructure and database event. Preserve seeds, model versions, game version, world snapshots, playerdata, SQLite events, known failures and experiment configurations. Never report an offline unit test as a full live Minecraft success.
13. **Adviser geography — nearby, evidence-based knowledge only.** If a civilization later develops a recognized leadership role with virtual advisers, those advisers may interpret known terrain and observations **around the settlement and its nearby explored surroundings**, not a complete world map. They provide **qualitative recommendations** (e.g., “there is a cave near the settlement worth exploring”), not secret resource coordinates, guaranteed ore deposits or omniscient answers. They must distinguish observed facts from hypotheses and cannot infer that unloaded/unknown areas are empty. The exact observation radius and data-refresh policy remain open engineering choices. Advisers do **not** spawn automatically on Day 1.
14. **Historical five-citizen Day 1 directive, not a V1 requirement.** In the older five-citizen single-settlement concept, citizens begin together with a common **starting objective**: survive, establish shelter, find food and **try to stay together long enough to form a functioning settlement**. This is a shared mission rather than an unbreakable command: citizens can cooperate, disagree, separate or revise their own goals when experience demands it. They begin **without a mayor or advisory council**. The experimenter's overarching research question remains stable; subsequent priorities should generally arise from citizen choices rather than the operator scripting “now form a government.” Any explicit human directive change is logged as an intervention and distinguished from emergent behavior.

15. **Staged construction and economy, not a first-demo blocker.** For the first public demo, AI citizens can independently build basic physical shelters and communicate requests for larger buildings, but no human contractor fulfills or finances construction during the uninterrupted autonomous run. After the demo, a separate logged human-contractor mode may sell large structures at substantial credit prices, with the human supplying construction materials and labor. The chosen economic foundation is individual wallets, an auditable ledger, a sell-only external resource market with limited demand and dynamic prices, and voluntary shared funding. Citizens may propose economic rules and social consequences; no scripted laws, tax seizures, compulsory contributions or punishments are automatically enforced. See the staged construction/economy design below.
16. **Controller architecture is replaceable when evidence favors it.** Evaluate PIANO and Astra/JEV concepts against the actual Windows implementation. Adopt focused improvements or a significant switch if repeatable real-world tests justify it. Retain independent cognition, memory and knowledge for each citizen; no central director scripts social outcomes. An additional model or full PIANO port is not required for the demo.
17. **YC Minecraft agent harness becomes our preferred execution-runtime candidate (October 31, 2026), NOT an adopted dependency yet.** The owner approves a safe, isolated experimental fork/prototype and head-to-head evaluation of [yc-software/minecraft-agent-harness](https://github.com/yc-software/minecraft-agent-harness). Prefer replacing or reusing our Minecraft body/runtime if verified to outperform the current system. Preserve original code and experiments until a replacement passes acceptance tests. The upstream system includes its own Pi-based cognition, not merely a drop-in body library; no assumption that our brain will integrate without changes.

## Initial personality model (design, not implemented)

| Axis (0–100) | Low end | High end | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work ethic | Avoids effort | Persistently diligent | Situational; not a compulsory job |
| Aggression | Non-confrontational | Confrontational | Does not mandate violence |
| Social trust | Suspicious | Trusting | Trust can differ by person |
| Empathy | Low concern for others | High concern for others | May conflict with self-interest |
| Curiosity | Prefers familiar things | Seeks novelty | May affect exploration |
| Risk tolerance | Risk-averse | Risk-seeking | Modulated by actual danger |
| Sociability | Solitary | Socially engaged | Not a friendship assignment |
| Leadership drive | Avoids leadership | Seeks influence | **Does not** grant mayor status |

**Generation contract:** sample one numeric value per independent dimension using a documented, weighted distribution (for example, middle values more common with occasional extremes); log the seed, version and generated values. Use contextual behavior and gradual learning, not hardcoded “aggression > 80 = murder” or “low work ethic = never gathers wood.” Check statistical diversity and avoid unintended duplicate/strongly correlated axes. Display adjectives only as descriptions of scores, not separate traits.

**Example:** work ethic 18, aggression 86, social trust 32, curiosity 75. This citizen may be suspicious and conflict-prone, but still choose cooperation when hungry or outnumbered. No action is predetermined by a number.

## Leadership and stronger-model access

**Historical five-citizen single-settlement concept (not a current first-demo or required follow-up):** five independent citizens, no mayor; each has its own beliefs, memories and ability to communicate. The settlement can publish objective shortages (e.g. food low) without commanding who must solve them.

**Initial shared directive (Day 1):** “You have arrived in an unfamiliar Minecraft world with four other citizens. Your immediate objective is to survive, establish shelter, find food, and try to form a functioning settlement together. You may cooperate, disagree, or change your plans as circumstances develop.” No hard-coded job assignments, government, or mandatory group movement. The initial survival mission may lead to new citizen-chosen objectives; externally imposed objective changes must be recorded as human interventions.

**Emergence:** citizens may propose organizations or leadership; election, acclamation, informal influence, coalition, coercion or refusal are all possible. A leadership claim should be grounded in witnessed speech/actions, commitments and actual recognition. Distinguish *claimant*, *recognized leader*, and *disputed office*.

**Privilege:** if an agreed social role earns a stronger planning model, route eligible role-specific high-level questions to that model using the same citizen identity and strictly perspective-limited context. There is no omniscient “mayor brain.” Ordinary citizen decisions remain independent. Measure differences caused by model strength as an experimental confound rather than calling them purely social emergence.

**Removal:** citizens may withdraw recognition, appoint another leader, split into factions or leave the office vacant. Physical death is separate from office succession. No implicit coronation, memory rewrite or transfer of a deceased citizen's personality.

**Information boundaries:** private speech is not mirrored into an omniscient shared citizen prompt. Observer audit logs are not accessible to actors in the simulation.

**Local geographic advice (future capability):** Virtual advisers, once available to a recognized leadership institution, work from authenticated observations and shared discoveries in the settlement's surrounding area. They may suggest a nearby cave, known pond or unexplored approach as a **possibility**, but cannot reveal exact hidden ore coordinates, claim knowledge of the whole map, or present an unverified guess as fact. Their advice remains optional; the mayor decides, and other citizens can refuse a proposed task. This is a design requirement, not implemented functionality.

Compare cost and reliability with API-mode baselines. Do not silently rotate providers/models, reuse one citizen's private context for another, or bypass provider-supported authentication.

### First-demo survival autonomy and reliability (locked design, not implemented)

**Option B — AI chooses strategies; scripts execute safely.** Every citizen's model decides its priorities and high-level survival plan (food, shelter, exploration, cooperation, when to flee). Deterministic Minecraft skills handle pathfinding, inventory operations, gathering/build actions, hazard checks, command validation, world-state verification and recovering from mechanical execution errors. The scripts must not routinely choose the answer to the food-and-shelter challenge or force citizen agreement. Do not present deterministic rescue actions as AI discoveries.

**Emergency shelter as a genuine last resort (approved exception).** A bounded deterministic emergency-protection reflex **may** activate if a citizen faces an immediate lethal threat and normal AI decision/execution cannot respond in time (for example, dangerous nighttime hostile contact with critical health). A physical, survival-legal temporary hideout or evasive maneuver is allowed **only if the player can perform it with present blocks/tools and actual safe terrain**: no teleporting, creative placement, free blocks, privileged commands, automatic permanent house construction, or hidden information. It must check hazards such as water, lava, falls and unstable blocks rather than blindly digging downward. Record trigger, attempt, real outcome, materials, and whether it was an automated fallback. Use conservative cooldowns so emergencies cannot silently become the default shelter algorithm. The exact trigger thresholds, safety protocol and metrics require controlled testing.

**Failure cooldowns — adopt and tune existing code.** Detect repeated identical errors (unreachable destination, missing local food, blocked path, invalid craft), temporarily suspend the exact failing action/target, surface the reason to the citizen for replanning and retry after context changes or a TTL expires. Use shorter windows for temporary conditions and longer ones for structural failures; never let stale blacklists permanently disable survival. **Preserve and benchmark existing Atlas backoff, navigation and hazard work; replace or reuse it only if the YC harness experiment demonstrates an improvement.** Track retries, TTL expirations and success/failure in run evidence.

**Filtered public activity bulletin — Option B, APPROVED FOR FIRST DEMO (design; not yet implemented).** Keep a **complete append-only Minecraft event and conversation history in SQLite for the human observer/debugger**, but **do not inject raw logs into citizens' prompts**. Each citizen instead receives a **perspective-aware, deterministic, target 150–250-token bulletin per strategic decision** containing only (a) relevant group/public chat addressed or accessible to that citizen, (b) publicly observable or intentionally shared settlement events, (c) the small current shared-state snapshot it may legitimately know (e.g. reported food supply, announced shelter status), and (d) a few recent important changes. This target applies to the **bulletin portion**, not the entire prompt; it is a tuning budget rather than a guarantee that critical events are discarded. The observer retains the full unfiltered audit log.

**Implement with inexpensive code, not an extra LLM summarizer:** tag each event with type, actor, timestamp, visibility/audience, location or settlement scope, source, and stable event ID; for each reader apply **access check → relevance/urgency ranking → repetition deduplication → deterministic aggregation → compact formatting**. For example, individual item pickups can become "Maya collected 12 logs" only where that information was witnessed or voluntarily published. Don't invent motives or summarize a suspicion as confirmed betrayal. Send **changes since the reader's last delivered event ID** plus a cached, compact state snapshot rather than resending the entire history. Persist facts that remain important after old events age out; include freshness/staleness and evidence references where practical.

**Privacy boundary:** Never expose another citizen's private thoughts, hidden goals, unshared inventory, private conversations, unseen locations, internal planning, or observer-only audit data merely because it exists in SQLite. In-world secrecy and legitimate independent discovery must remain possible. Use only intentionally communicated and observable public information in the first demo; richer private channels and surveillance are later separate decisions. **Measure actual bulletin token counts, omitted-event reasons, and coordination outcomes**; urgent warnings may temporarily exceed the 150–250-token target if explicitly recorded. No omniscient default team bulletin.

### Post-demo civilization economy and human-contractor system — agreed design, not implemented

**Scope and purpose:** The economy exists to create meaningful individual wealth, scarcity, trade-offs, voluntary cooperation and possible social conflict, while avoiding a second giant project. Implement a small **TypeScript + SQLite** economic service; let the **existing autonomous citizens** make the social and purchasing decisions. No separate mandatory government AI, banker AI, market NPC or complex physical exchange building. Market and contractor functions are world infrastructure, **not a hidden AI leader**. This entire system is **post-demo**, unless a tiny logging hook already exists and imposes no first-demo dependency.

**1. Individual wallets and voluntary collective funding (approved):** Each citizen has a persistent credit balance and independently decides to save, transfer, donate, lend or contribute to a communal construction fund. Use a durable append-only, auditable transaction ledger with unique transaction IDs. Shared money only moves after an authorized, verified transfer; a citizen cannot spend somebody else's wallet or automatically impose a tax because it says so. A communal fund has its own ledger balance. The model is monetary bookkeeping, not a substitute for Minecraft's actual physical item ownership.

**2. Sell-only external resource marketplace first (approved):** A citizen can ask to sell actual resources in its owned Minecraft inventory. Validate ownership and quantity, calculate a quote, check available external demand and the market's current buying budget, verify acceptance, remove the purchased inventory items from the Minecraft world as an **export**, then credit the citizen's wallet and record the trade. No physical market building, buyer NPC, buyer warehouse, item resale or unlimited market inventory simulation. For the baseline economy the **external market buys items only**; purchasing Minecraft goods from an external market is a **possible later feature**, not an initial requirement. Citizen-to-citizen resource trade can be added incrementally but does not require an external purchase system.

**3. Scarcity, prices and currency controls (approved):** Prices depend on supply, demand and scarcity, not a permanent fixed exchange such as "every wheat is always worth five credits." An oversupplied wheat market pays progressively less and eventually may decline additional purchases; genuine shortages may raise prices. **Declining prices alone are insufficient**: enforce finite market demand, limited buyer budgets, controlled replenishment and budget persistence across pause/resume. Model currency issuance explicitly through verified market payments and **remove contractor fees from circulation permanently** as a currency sink. Track supply, payouts, spending, current money stock and price indexes so commodity depreciation is not confused with general monetary inflation. Do not reset prices, demand or purchasing capacity after a server restart or human construction session. Avoid promising a perfectly realistic inflation model.

**4. Human construction contractor (approved post-demo):** Citizens may independently request expensive, larger buildings (warehouses, bridges, houses, public infrastructure), specifying their purpose and preferred known location. **Pricing starts as deterministic, auditable code** based on building scope, complexity, labor and market conditions. Quotes should be **substantial** relative to wealth so construction requires genuine choices, but not intentionally impossible. Citizens receive the quote and freely accept, reject, negotiate scope, pool voluntary credits, save, or decide to build something themselves. **Payment is one credit amount—not a required list of wheat, wood, iron or other supplied resources.** The human contractor supplies the materials and physically builds the contracted structure in an explicitly logged construction mode. The agreed contractor payment is burned/removed from total credit circulation; do not credit a fake human wallet and circulate it back by default. Record approval, payment/reservation, work performed, construction materials introduced, completion and inspection. Do not complete unpaid or unapproved requests.

**5. Async availability handled with session checkpoints, not 24/7 human labor (approved):** Citizens may issue requests during active simulation, but only a small bounded number of approved projects may be outstanding to avoid unbounded work queues. At a controlled, safe session checkpoint, save the Minecraft world plus citizens' identity/memory, task state, wallets, open contracts, ledger and market state. Stop citizen decisions and gameplay for the construction phase; ensure Minecraft game time cannot silently advance while the world is supposed to be frozen, or document any actual world-time progression. The owner completes approved construction whenever available, then resumes the next simulation session. The 60-minute **first public demo stays continuous and never pauses for human work**. Report contractor interventions separately; on resumption citizens discover the results through normal observation/public announcements rather than an omniscient prompt.

**6. Emergent social rules and consequences (approved):** Citizens can propose communal taxes, obligations, resource-sharing agreements or sanctions, agree or dissent, and remember disputes; **no preinstalled governing role, obligatory tax percentages, forced voting consensus or automatically executed punishment**. Refusal to fund construction may prompt freely chosen negotiation, generosity, exclusion, confiscation attempts, exile attempts or actual Minecraft combat, but no result is scripted or guaranteed. Any attempted taking, access restriction or violence must be represented by permitted in-world Minecraft actions with the target's opportunity to resist; deaths follow the experiment's configured demo-respawn or later permadeath policy. The engine records agreements and actual outcomes rather than declaring an AI's threat executed.

**7. Pricing intelligence and implementation phasing:** Begin with deterministic formulas for both market and contractor pricing; **defer a fully AI-controlled pricing model** until after repeatable economic runs. Keep a small pluggable pricing interface so a future LLM may propose/negotiation quotes, but all actual fund transfers, supply limits, sale validation and price acceptance remain enforced by reliable code. Do not train a custom pricing model for the first iteration.

**8. Transaction safety and measurement:** Inventory removal and SQLite money credit are not magically one atomic Minecraft+database transaction; use durable pending states, unique idempotency keys, verified effects, retries/reconciliation and crash recovery so interruptions do not mint duplicate credits or permanently consume items without an accountable settlement. Record pricing inputs and quote versions, sale quantity, credits created/burned, buyer demand and budget, inflation indicators, contract backlog, user intervention, world-state changes and citizen decisions. **Never treat human-provided construction as an autonomously built AI structure.**

**Explicit staged rollout:** (1) **First autonomous demo**—simple agent building and citizen-originated *unfulfilled* requests, no wallets/market/contractor fulfillment required. (2) **Post-demo economic foundation and contractor sessions**—wallets, sell-only exports, dynamic finite-demand pricing, signed-off contracts, payment burning, checkpoint/build/resume; add one reliable feature at a time. (3) **Later extensions**—AI-controlled quote setting, market purchases, richer citizen-to-citizen trades, economic institutions, advanced surveillance/politics and long-term controlled social experiments. This phase plan is intentional scope reduction, not evidence these features are already shipped.

After the first public demo, continue improving reliability and adding genuinely tested capabilities through repeatable simulation rounds. Record what citizens actually did, failures, new mechanisms, model/tool changes and limitations; share follow-up GitHub/LinkedIn progress only with the owner's approval. Keep optional features (government, virtual advisers, surveillance, colonies at scale, extended economy) out of the initial critical path unless they are necessary to demonstrate independent survival and interaction.

**Public-record intelligence only for the initial adviser/surveillance concept (future, not implemented):** if an advisory institution eventually emerges, a settlement intelligence function may summarize legitimately observable **public** activity—communal storage changes, resource contributions, publicly witnessed disputes, voluntarily shared reports and abandoned commitments. It must cite the underlying event records, distinguish facts from inferences and avoid reading private conversations, secret thoughts or observer-only audit logs. No physical spy bots or omniscient digital interception in this approved first scope; any wider surveillance system remains an unresolved later discussion, not a coding requirement.

**Research direction after the first demo:** Repeat and counterbalance the locked **2v2 GPT/Claude** experiment before interpreting model differences; then consider **3v3**, longer trials and gradual scaling toward 10, 20 and beyond only after successful live reliability tests. Additional providers (Grok/Gemini) and controlled separate-model comparisons are later research, not V1 dependencies. The old three-then-five-citizen single-settlement sequence is historical, not an active gate. Colonies, trade and governance are interesting **potential emergent outcomes**, not objectives the prompt forces into existence.

**Minimal per-citizen architecture to verify or prototype, in existing TypeScript stack:**
1. **Perception/inbox (nonblocking):** Mineflayer observes game state, nearby threats, authorized speech and physical results; incoming messages/events are buffered by each citizen's visibility rules. Do not stall a running Minecraft skill for a slow LLM response. No default global telepathic state.
2. **Independent memory/social/strategic state:** Each citizen maintains its own intent, traits, memories, conversational commitments, observations and current plan. Background planning may update objectives while action execution continues. A lightweight memory lookup and event queue suffice at first; do **not** require a vector database or separate LLM for memory, social and planning.
3. **Cognitive controller (coherence bottleneck):** At safe action boundaries, reconcile the current goal, health/hazard priority, latest valid plan, incoming chat, resource constraints and known commitments. Choose or authorize one next action and any conversational response without allowing a stale plan to overwrite newer information. The same action pipeline must record whether a promise was knowingly broken, forgotten, blocked or never attempted. **Coherence is not forced moral obedience:** an AI may deliberately lie, betray, refuse, change its mind or ignore another citizen, provided it acts from its own information and the result is logged accurately.
4. **Closed legal action catalog and physical execution:** The controller can only pick currently feasible actions exposed by deterministic Mineflayer skills; code validates legality, inventory, proximity, danger and timeouts. Separate high-level intent from low-level movement/building, verify all physical outcomes and apply bounded retry/backoff/TTL on repeated failures. Emergency reflexes are explicitly bounded and separately logged, never fabricated as AI ingenuity.
5. **Async lifecycle and observability:** Attach a version/ID to each plan and action; discard superseded, expired or wrong-stage completions, cancel or safely finish stale jobs, and preserve durable memory/event logs. Instrument planning latency, controller latency, execution latency, costs, token counts, throughput, contradictory decisions, action success rate, deaths, TPS and stall time. Run one active executor per citizen; asynchronous cognition must not create simultaneous conflicting Mineflayer commands.

**Three implementation options to benchmark, not blindly commit:**
- **Baseline:** Existing TypeScript decision pipeline + deterministic legal action skills, fixed where broken. This is the control condition.
- **PIANO-lite:** Existing LLM for strategic priorities and meaningful conversation, but nonblocking perception/planning, per-citizen intention reconciliation and legal deterministic execution. Prefer **this** as the first low-cost candidate if the audit identifies actual stale/blocked/conflicting decisions. Use native Node/TypeScript async machinery and SQLite; no Python bridge, Redis/MQTT/Ray/Mosec, extra controller model or cloud infra by default.
- **Planner + fast-model controller:** An Astra/JEV-style two-model pipeline is optional if benchmarks show measurable reliability, responsiveness or **total dollar-cost** gains over PIANO-lite. Any fast LLM still incurs latency/tokens; a deterministic controller may be cheaper and safer. A separate controller may choose means, but each citizen's higher-level intentions, conversation and private memory stay independent. The model used in a speedrun or provided through a developer tool is **not automatically** available or free through the consumer subscription.

**Decision gate to approve the architecture switch:** Audit real Windows code first. Identify specific failures: LLM stalls, social/action contradictions, stale plans, invalid selections, hazard reaction delay, repeated loops, multi-bot cross-talk. Write a focused failing test or trace for each. Compare baseline versus candidate on the same isolated World-Lab conditions, including **live** Minecraft runs and cost/latency; offline tests are supporting evidence only. Integrate an approach if it fixes important blockers without unacceptable regressions, expense, privacy leakage or demonstrator bias. If Atlas is drowning because of pathfinding, breath handling or terrain hazards, fix those physical causes rather than assuming a neural controller will solve them. Keep the heavy full PIANO clone and additional multi-LLM concurrency as post-demo research unless justified by an immediate verified blocker.
